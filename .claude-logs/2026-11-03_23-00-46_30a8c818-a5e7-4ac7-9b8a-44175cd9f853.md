The retention steps are enough to be remembered, and too dry to be liked. Play belongs in this app. It belongs inside the class, as Maya and Omar, rather than as a game next to the lesson.

Someone who already studies will come back for a due gap. Someone who pasted a YouTube link out of curiosity will do that once, meet another quiz, and leave. Home today is a link box and a card. The class is explain, four questions, a score. Voice and the pixel students make one session nicer. They do not give the next visit a personality.

What does not fit, even as flavor, is streaks, XP, badges, a shop, or a separate mini-game. A 5-day review would break a daily streak, and a points layer would become the thing people chase. TeachBack already has that. A joke that ignores the video would also fall flat, because the whole product is built on the transcript being the truth.

The playful version of the same loop:

- **Someone is already at the board.** Home opens on Maya or Omar with today’s one question half-written. The link box stays underneath. Opening the app is walking into a room where a person is waiting.
- **They mishear you.** The second warm-up on a gap is Maya repeating yesterday’s answer with one wrong turn (“so the mitochondria *are* the cell?”). You fix that piece. Same schedule, same one minute, and catching the mistake is more fun than being quizzed.
- **A mood you can see.** Open gaps leave that student confused or skeptical. When the gap closes, the hand drops and a lime chalk mark lands. No number goes up. People come back to see a face change.
- **One unscored line after class.** Maya’s bad analogy or Omar’s dry remark, using something you actually said. It is still on the board the next time you open the app.
- **How they talk to you shifts** after a few gaps close. Maya asks shorter questions. Omar stops opening with what you skipped. No meter on screen.

The first build is the note, one question a day, and the student already on the board. Memory, the mishearing, and the close come next, as one scene: they remember what you said, they get it wrong, they finally get it.

Shayan’s notes are right about three things. A “gap is due” email is a reminder for a chore, and it cannot be shown on Thursday without the platform keys. A stats card reassures people who are already back. And once someone stops pasting videos, the warm-ups run out. A daily 3-minute teach fixes the empty first screen and gives people a reason to open the app today. It also needs a video picked every day, a streak, and a share that nobody has proven yet. That is more than the time you have, and it changes the app from “teach the video you watched” into “teach our video.”

The chalkboard is the small push that still does the retention job:

- Home is the board of ideas they have taught.
- Each open idea dims as its due time gets closer. It fades. It does not disappear, so it does not feel like a punishment.
- The faintest idea is the one warm-up. Tapping it opens that question, with the line they said last time.
- A right answer writes it bright again. Two rights and it stays.
- The link box stays on the board.
